In a recent publication,
[**Chiara**](/members/animali), [**Pierre**](/members/auclair) and
[**Baptiste**](/members/blachier), together with [**Vincent
Vennin**](https://www.lpens.ens.psl.eu/vincent-vennin/?lang=en),
have developed a novel framework to implement stochastic inflation on
stochastic trees. These trees represent dynamically how different
regions of space grow and gradually become causally disconnected.

Cosmic Inflation is an hypothetical early phase of accelerated
expansion that has occurred before the first billionth of a second of
existence of our Universe. It provides a natural mechanism to seed the
galaxies of today by the quantum fluctuations that are inherently
sourced during the inflationary era (see this [**post**]({% post_url
2022-09-15-2205.12608 %})).

Traditionally, we model these perturbations using a **perturbative**
approach, assuming they are small enough not to significantly affect
the background metric. However, if perturbations grow large enough,
they start influencing the overall structure of spacetime and this is
where non-perturbative methods come into play.

**Stochastic inflation** is a non-perturbative treatment that models
cosmic inflation as a stochastic process, much like a random walk.
This approach allows for a more realistic description of the
universe's dynamics during inflation, capturing both quantum diffusion
and backreaction effects.

---

In this context, we have developed a new framework based on the
[**Langevin's
equation**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langevin_equation) to
generate stochastic trees, an example of which being given below.

![atree](/assets/images/2501.05371/laplacian_04_tree.gv.jpg "A stochastic tree generated by FOREST")

These trees allow for the generation of real-space maps of the
curvature perturbation that capture quantum diffusion and its
non-perturbative backreaction during inflation. In our approach, we do
not need to assume a fixed background, new spacetime units emerge
dynamically as the trees unfold, naturally incorporating the metric
fluctuations.

To implement this framework computationally, we have developed the
FOrtran Recursive Exploration of Stochastic Trees (FOREST) code, which
is presented in Ref. {% cite animali:2025pyf %}. FOREST is designed to
efficiently explore and simulate these stochastic trees, providing a
powerful platform for studying inflationary dynamics.

---

One of the most exciting applications of our method concerns the
formation of primordial black holes (PBH) from large curvature
fluctuations. In Ref. {% cite animali:2025pyf %}, we discuss why, and
how, PBHs can form at the unbalanced nodes in the stochastic trees and
FOREST allows us to estimate their mass distribution. The following
figure shows a two-dimensional comoving map tracing the fluctuations
of the curvature perturbation due to strong quantum effects. These
lead to the formation of five PBH (dark regions).

![map](/assets/images/2501.05371/laplacian_04_no_pattern.png "2D comoving map with five PBHs")

Applied to the "quantum-well" model, a toy setting in which stochastic
inflation takes place, we find the PBHs to have masses following a
mild power law terminated by exponential tails. The following figure
shows the mass distribution as a function of the size of the quantum
well $$\mu$$.

![hist](/assets/images/2501.05371/hist_pbhs.png "Mass distribution of formed PBHs")
